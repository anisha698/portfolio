NASA × Parsons, External Engagement Studio

Communication Strategies for the ISS retirement

RoleResearch & prototyping · mapping · campaign · leadership
TimelineSpring 2025 · presented to NASA, May 2025
Studio15 weeks · 16-person team
MentorsMark Kroeckel (Parsons); Christie Cox & Lynn Harper (NASA)

A 15-week Parsons studio in collaboration with NASA. The ISS retires in 2030 after 25 years of continuous human presence, and a new commercial era in Low Earth Orbit begins. NASA’s challenge wasn’t scientific, it was a storytelling problem: how do you build public investment in something most people don’t realize they already depend on?

My role

Research and prototyping, as part of a 16-person studio. I focused on stakeholder and user mapping, the foundation the whole strategy was built on, and on developing the A day in the life without NASA campaign. Leadership rotated through the team, and I stepped up to steer and align us when it was my turn to lead.

01 / 07

The brief

Tell the story of the extraordinary science done on the ISS, and the expanded opportunity landscape beyond it in LEO.

02 / 07

The gap

Space quietly shapes daily life, weather forecasting, water filtration, food safety, cancer detection, yet the story only ever reaches scientists and policymakers. Our research surfaced three gaps: hidden achievements, an audience gap, and a funding imbalance. Space was improving life on Earth, and almost no one outside the field knew it.

03 / 07

Mapping the system (my focus)

Before we could tell the story, we had to know who it was for. I helped chart the full ecosystem of groups who shape, experience, and are impacted by space, then distilled that sprawl into six audiences we’d actually design for, deliberately centering the ones usually left out: students, creative professionals, and underrepresented communities.

That map became the spine of everything. The six strategic domains, the three-phase roadmap, and all eight prototypes trace directly back to it, which is exactly why getting it right mattered.

04 / 07

A day in the life without NASA (the campaign I built)

The insight is simple and a little ruthless: people don’t mobilize over data, they mobilize over loss. So instead of listing what space gave us, we took it away. A counterfactual campaign, timed to NASA’s 25th anniversary of continuous human presence in space, that walks you through an ordinary morning and quietly deletes everything tracing back to space research. The weather alert that never warns you. The GPS that doesn’t exist. The water filter, the food safety standard, the early cancer screening, gone, one by one.

By the end, the day isn’t just inconvenient. It’s unrecognizable. That’s the whole strategy: an emotional shock of recognition. You can’t feel grateful for something you never noticed, so we made people feel its absence first.

05 / 07

The system

We didn’t design eight separate ideas, we designed one system. Eight prototypes across three time horizons (build awareness · carry the transition · sustain a thriving future), each feeding audiences into the others to compound reach.

06 / 07

What I took from it

The design work taught me a lot, but the team taught me more. Sixteen people, six domains, ninety-plus ideas, and a lot of strong opinions in one room. With leadership rotating, there were moments it was on me to read where the team actually was, cut through the noise, and pull us back to a shared direction. Learning when to push my own idea and when to champion someone else’s is the thing I carried out of this studio most.

07 / 07

The outcome

We didn’t hand NASA a single deliverable, we handed them a direction. A system for a thriving and just space future: not one intervention, but many, reinforcing each other across time. And it starts with making people feel that space was always theirs.
